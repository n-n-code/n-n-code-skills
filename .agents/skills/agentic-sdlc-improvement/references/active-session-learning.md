# Active-session learning and promotion

Read this when an active development task yields a reusable finding, when an
authorized candidate may be installed, or when the improver itself is under
study. This is a workflow contract, not a scheduler or permission grant.

## Capture an opportunity without creating a second task

Delivery owns the requested outcome. This skill owns a warranted improvement
experiment. A material observation can be a failure, a successful recovery, an
unexpectedly effective approach, or a workaround made obsolete by new evidence.
Retain its source handle, affected decision, plausible explanation, contrary
evidence, and likely owner. Do not manufacture a lesson from every successful
task or turn one incident into a universal instruction.

Use a conversation record or the host's authorized task-artifact storage. Keep
run logs, transcripts, result tables, and per-run hashes outside the skill source
repository. Reusable fixtures and concise technical compatibility facts can live
with the skill; fixtures remain synthetic until separately executed and graded.
Memory has its own authority boundary and is not an automatic destination.

Estimate the likely value from consequence and expected reuse. Choose a cheap
test that could change the decision before launching a larger comparison. Stop
when evidence is decisive, the expected information no longer justifies the
cost, progress stalls, or an applicable hard limit is reached. Do not impose a
universal number of attempts, cases, agents, or minutes.

## Own the right change

Distinguish a task defect from a reusable workflow defect. Route wording,
context, tooling, test strategy, and skill packaging to their owners while this
skill retains responsibility for the comparison. Preserve working behavior;
consider removing or consolidating instructions as well as adding them. Change
one meaningful module when practical, then check its integration with the rest
of the workflow. More detailed instructions and more autonomous execution are
candidate choices, not goals in themselves.

Separate three decisions:

- **Behavioral improvement:** requires paired baseline/candidate outcomes on the
  target, fresh analogous cases, and protected behavior before promotion as an
  improvement. Declare benefit only for the conditions actually tested.
- **Maintenance:** a broken link, invalid metadata, stale tool contract, or other
  objective invariant can be repaired against that invariant. Verify affected
  behavior where needed; do not present the repair as measured efficacy.
- **Bootstrap:** new evaluation or learning machinery can be installed when
  authorized after checking its capability and integrity. Label its effect on
  development outcomes unmeasured until there is a valid comparison.

## Promotion belongs to the host's authorized process

Before mutation, identify the editable artifact set, the source of authority,
the accepted evaluation contract, and the destination. Preserve existing grants;
do not ask again for covered actions. If authority covers proposals only, finish
the reviewable candidate and evidence without installing it.

Keep acceptance thresholds, protected graders/cases, permission policy, budget
accounting, and promotion/rollback helpers outside the candidate's writable
surface. A candidate may propose a separately reviewed correction to a defective
oracle; apply an independently justified correction to both comparison arms.
It may not change these controls to pass its own evaluation. A Markdown rule is
guidance, not an enforced filesystem boundary; disclose what the host actually
enforces.

Record both source and active installation identities before testing or replacing
a skill. A source edit does not prove that the active agent loaded it. Avoid
overwriting local installation drift or an unrelated source edit. Use the host's
approved installation/promotion mechanism, verify the installed identity, and
keep a recoverable predecessor. Do not edit provider-managed skill caches or
silently replace a different version. Report source-only, tested, and installed
states separately.

When the host uses the bundled local utility, follow
[local promotion and recovery](promotion.md) for its policy, request interface,
serialized writes, and interrupted-operation recovery. The utility does not grade
the evidence or establish an agent runtime.

Keep an index of useful accepted, rejected, and inconclusive interventions with
their conditions and evidence handles. Preserve failed attempts within their
experiment; retrieve history selectively instead of adding all of it to every
task. Reuse an older successful module when current evidence supports it. A new
model, tool interface, or host version can invalidate earlier efficacy claims;
rerun relevant comparison cases before extending those claims to the new pairing.

## Improve the improver only when indicated

Repeated rejected proposals, demonstrably wrong diagnoses, or stagnation can
justify investigating this workflow itself. Ordinary product failures alone do
not show that the improver is defective. In the absence of such evidence, leave
this loop dormant.

Freeze the initial target skills, available history, tasks, evaluator, model and
tool conditions, and resource allowance for both improvers. Run each from an
independent copy of that state. Compare the downstream quality of their proposed
or promoted changes, including regressions, rejections, and cost when measured.
A prettier experiment report is not a better improver. Do not let the second arm
inherit the first arm's answers, improved skill, or consumed holdouts.

The candidate improver cannot change its own permission policy, graders,
promotion gates, or helper implementation. Changes to those controls are a
separate authorized maintenance task with independent validation. If matched
evaluation is unavailable, retain a proposal and its uncertainty rather than
claiming a recursive improvement.
